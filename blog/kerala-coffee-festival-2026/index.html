Kerala Coffee Festival 2026 and Kozhikode’s Coffee Weekend

Competitions on Beach Road. Visiting brewers at café counters. Keralam-grown coffees in the cup.

By Lex Hamood · Home brewer & content creator
Published 13 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026 · The 7–8 November 2026 edition

On 7 and 8 November 2026, Kerala Coffee Festival brought brewing competitions, workshops and coffee businesses to Aspin Courtyards on Beach Road, Kozhikode. Across the city, independent café events added international coffees, visiting brewers and Keralam-grown lots to the same weekend.

For anyone following specialty coffee in Malabar, the story reached beyond one venue. The festival programme sat alongside an Omakasé collaboration involving Sukoru and Bermito, a NAS × Jaseem bar takeover, Underblu’s coffee selection and Urban Hippie’s Canephora cupping. Together, they offer a closer look at Kozhikode’s growing coffee community.

Kerala Coffee Festival 2026: three ways to compete

The festival’s published programme brought roasters, cafés, brewers and equipment businesses into one setting. Three competitions covered filter brewing, espresso service and sensory recognition. The 2026 festival archive records the dates and venue.

Hario V60 Brewers Cup India — Calicut chapter

Hario’s Calicut chapter was scheduled for 7 November, from 9 AM to 5 PM. Ekata Coffee Roasters was named as coffee partner, Kerala Coffee Festival as regional organiser and Rotana Motors as venue sponsor. The competition centred on manual filter brewing with the V60.

Barista Championship presented by Kaapi Solutions

The 8 November Barista Championship programme set a ten-minute performance: competitors were asked to prepare two espressos and two milk or latte-art drinks. The format specified two sensory judges and one technical judge.

In its post-event report, Kaapi Solutions named Régine Léonie, Jowin Joy and Jubunu K.R. as the judging panel and described the championship as powered by Astoria. The report congratulated the winner without naming them; a verified winner’s name is not yet included here.

Metrovaartha’s report from Kozhikode also identifies the Barista Championship as a highlight of the weekend. KAAPI Solutions displayed professional coffee equipment at stall A7.

Aroma Master Championship

Aroma Master, listed for 8 November, focused on recognising and distinguishing aromas. Its advertised rounds moved from identification to naming, odd-one-out and aroma-card matching tasks. The rules allowed up to 50 initial entrants and 12 finalists; these were format limits, not confirmed attendance figures.

Workshops and people on the programme

The workshop listings offered several starting points, from a first introduction to coffee to more detailed brewing practice:

  • 7 November — Coffee Through the Senses: green coffee, processing, roasting, tasting vocabulary and guided cupping.
  • 8 November — Beyond the Brew, with Matin Shaikh: pour-over technique, grind, water, temperature, flow and extraction.
  • 8 November — The Signature Lab, with Tejas: flavour combinations and signature beverages.
  • 8 November — Coffee From Zero, with Abhil: a Malayalam introduction to beans, roasting, aroma and practical brewing.

Hario’s published Calicut judging panel named Soudh Ibrahim, Akanksha Gupta, Prajwal, Ajay Girish Redij, Felix Daniel and Matin Shaikh.

Brew bar, blind tastings and roasting

The festival brew-bar terms described three stations and a communal electric grinder, with brewed coffee to be shared free. It offered space to introduce a coffee or a brewing method outside the competition format.

BerryCo’s Stall A3 announcements included blind tastings on both days, an Instagram-to-stall coffee knowledge challenge, Coffee Unfiltered on 7 November and a roasting demonstration on 8 November. Other named experiences explored water, crema, aeration and fermentation.

Sukoru, Bermito and the Omakasé experience

Alongside the festival weekend, Sukoru, Bermito Coffee Roasters and Crafted Coffee Experience collaborated on an Omakasé event with NAS. The 7 November announcement offered two sessions, with six seats per session, at ₹1,999 per person.

The poster introduced NAS through his 2019 Bahrain AeroPress and 2024 Saudi Brewers Cup titles. International coffees featured in the experience, according to the local account supplied for this article. The advertised six-person sessions gave the event a small-table format, distinct from the festival’s exhibition programme.

NAS × Jaseem: Kerala Origins coffees at Sukoru

On 8 November, NAS and Jaseem took over the bar at Sukoru with Kerala Origins coffees roasted by Bermito Coffee Roasters. The session was advertised for 10 AM–1 PM. View the NAS × Jaseem takeover archive for the original poster and event details.

That credit follows the coffee through different parts of its preparation: Keralam-grown lots, roasting by Bermito in Kozhikode, and brewing by NAS and Jaseem at the café. Kerala Origins works to make the state’s producers and their coffees better known through evaluation, processing, roasting and opportunities to taste together.

Kerala Origins and Kerala Coffee Festival are separate initiatives. This takeover was independently organised during the same weekend. Readers interested in the coffees themselves can explore our guide to Keralam coffee producers and the people behind the beans.

Underblu and Urban Hippie’s cupping

Vajid of Bermito and Kerala Origins also reports opening activity at Underblu around the weekend, accompanied by a premium coffee selection. Underblu now operates independently. Its earlier connection with Southwave is part of the city’s coffee history: Southwave’s account records Under Blu as its in-store café during that earlier chapter, alongside fashion, art and music.

Vajid’s account includes a Canephora-focused cupping by Urban Hippie Coffee Roasters, described as Canephora Culture. A cupping is a structured tasting in which coffees are compared under similar preparation conditions. Here, attention turned to the species widely associated with the name Robusta.

Urban Hippie founder and roaster Koyan Althani describes experience across farming, processing, green grading, cupping and production roasting. The roastery’s own account lists coffee-tasting salons and cuppings among its activities.
