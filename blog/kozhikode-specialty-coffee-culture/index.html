Kerala’s specialty coffee boom: what’s happening in Kozhikode?

Coffee runs, music gatherings, shared tasting tables and local roasters are giving Calicut’s coffee culture more places to grow.

A city gathering around coffee

A coffee scene becomes interesting when people start gathering around it. In Kozhikode, also known as Calicut, that gathering takes several forms: a running community with coffee in its identity, a room tasting Kerala-grown coffees, a brewing competition or a café bringing music and coffee together.

These activities offer a useful way to understand the specialty coffee boom in Malabar. The change is not simply a matter of more café menus. Coffee is becoming part of how people meet, learn and spend time in the city.

Underblu at Southwave: coffee meets the creative crowd

Underblu at Southwave is an important part of this connection. Southwave’s Calicut experience store opened in June 2024. Its published story describes Under Blu as the café inside a space bringing streetwear, art, music and community events together. [2]

The audience has grown beyond a quiet café visit. In November 2025, The New Indian Express reported on Kozhikode’s coffee-brew parties, where specialty coffee accompanied DJs and alcohol-free gatherings. Underblu founder Shahal Ali described attendance growing from about 20 people at an early meet to more than 150, with five editions held by the time of the report. [1]

That is one organiser’s experience, but it shows a route into coffee that does not begin with equipment or technical language. Someone can come for the music, try a different coffee and return wanting to know more.

Coffee runs bring a different crowd

Kozhikode’s coffee culture also overlaps with its running community. Brew Burn Run Club places coffee and movement within the same social setting. Its own account includes runs, cycling, kayaking and coffee raves among its community activities. [3]

A running club is not automatically a specialty-coffee event. What matters here is the overlap: people who might not attend a formal tasting still have a reason to meet around coffee. The conversation can begin after a run, without anyone needing to know a farm name or brewing recipe first.

Cupping gives curiosity somewhere to go

For people who want to explore the cup more closely, workshops and cuppings provide another way in. Bermito’s event archive includes Bean Curious, a beginner-focused workshop with Sukoru, and Asia to Cup, which combined Southeast Asian coffees with guided cupping and live roasting. [4]

Kerala Origins adds a local focus. Its first gathering at Sukoru in Kozhikode brought Kerala-grown coffees, producer conversations and a brew bar into the same room. Its archive includes Windermere, On Ground Farm, Silent Valley, Terra Vagamon, Krishnagiri and Puliyarmala. [5]

Tasting coffees alongside one another gives visitors something concrete to discuss. Instead of being told that two origins are different, they can compare the cups and ask questions. The names of producers and places start to mean more when there is a coffee to remember them by.

Roasters bring another part of coffee closer

Bermito Coffee Roasters, founded by brothers Vajid and Hadi Roshan, roasts in Kozhikode. Its small-batch coffees from Indian and international origins sit alongside its workshops and tasting programmes. Local roasting gives customers a closer connection to the people making decisions about the beans they buy. [6]

Koyan Althani, founder and roaster at Urban Hippie Coffee Roasters, brings experience across farming, processing, grading, roasting and brewing. His published biography records a first-runner-up finish in the Indian Coffee Roasting Championship, alongside his training and workshop work. [7]

The value of that experience extends beyond a competition result. It can help café teams and home brewers understand why a coffee behaves differently, how to prepare it and what to look for when tasting.

Calicut has a place in competitive brewing

The Indian AeroPress Championship’s official Calicut chapter listing names The Long House Collective in Kozhikode as its venue and Ekata Coffee Roasters as the coffee supplier. It places the city within the championship’s regional programme. [8]

Competitions give brewers a shared task and a reason to practise together. For spectators, they reveal how much attention can go into a familiar drink. For participants, the conversations around the competition can be as useful as the result.

AeroPress brewing, espresso-based barista performance and roasting are distinct disciplines. Recognising those differences gives each achievement its proper place in the city’s coffee culture.

Festivals connect the wider coffee community

Kerala Coffee Festival brings roasters, brewers, cafés and coffee drinkers into the wider conversation. Its focus on coffee experiences and participation makes it a different kind of gathering from the producer-focused work of Kerala Origins. [9]

Festivals offer the chance to encounter several coffees and businesses in one setting. Smaller cuppings and workshops allow more time for a particular subject. A growing community benefits from both: a welcoming first encounter and opportunities to keep learning.

Why this matters for Malabar

Taken together, these gatherings, local roasters and brewing activities suggest a coffee community with several points of entry. Kozhikode’s contribution lies in the connections between these spaces: a café visit can lead to a tasting, a workshop or a conversation with a roaster.

Arayie’s early coffee community, Underblu at Southwave, Sukoru’s tasting tables and local roasting work belong to different parts of that picture. Our Kerala specialty coffee story explores the earlier connections, including Arayie and Ekata. The producer guide follows the conversation back to the farms.

For a visitor, the best starting point is whatever makes you curious: a café, a cupping or a bag of beans. Explore the Kozhikode coffee directory, ask who roasted the coffee and find out who grew it. That is how a busy coffee scene becomes a community people keep returning to.
