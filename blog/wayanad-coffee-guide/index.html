Wayanad coffee: meet the region behind the cup

Get to know Wayanad Robusta and plan a considerate visit to Kerala’s coffee-growing region, with links to cafés and producers.

Specialty Coffee Kerala Map · 19 September 2026

A coffee-growing landscape

Wayanad is an important part of Kerala’s coffee story. The Coffee Board of India identifies Robusta as the region’s main coffee type and describes coffee grown alongside crops such as pepper and banana. The region is a place of working farms and livelihoods as well as a destination for visitors.

A regional name tells you where to begin asking questions, but it cannot describe every cup. Two coffees from Wayanad can differ in variety, processing, roast and preparation. Ask for the producer or estate name when you want to understand a coffee more closely.

Taste first, then explore

For a café visit, the directory includes Wishlist Coffee in Kalpetta. It also lists producers and estates under a separate farm category, including Old Babbler Coffee in Meppadi. Use those links to learn what each business currently offers. A producer listing does not necessarily mean a walk-in café or an organised tour.

If you try a coffee you enjoy, note the origin and processing information on the bag or menu. Ask how the café prepares it and what the producer recommends for home brewing. Your preference matters more than trying to match somebody else’s tasting description.

Farm visits need an invitation

Contact the estate before planning a visit. Ask whether visitors are accepted, whether a guide is available and which parts of the farm can be accessed. Keep to the meeting point and route the host provides. Harvesting, processing and other work may take priority over visits.

Some entries on this map describe private estates or regional producer networks. They deliberately do not include precise public map pins. A network covering several farms is not a single destination. Use the organisation’s own contact route instead of guessing an entrance from a general map.

Bring a useful question home

When choosing coffee, look for information you can use again: the producer, the coffee name and the roast date. Ask the seller to suggest a grind for the equipment you actually use. If you already have beans at home, compare one small purchase at a time and make notes rather than changing everything in your recipe at once.

Explore Wayanad on the map to see the current listings, and check the Events page for opportunities to meet producers or join a tasting. Events can be a convenient way to learn about several coffees without assuming that every farm offers public visits.
